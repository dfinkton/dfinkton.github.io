## The framing, which reorganises the essay
This is not a separate practice sitting beside distress tolerance and acceptance. It is what they are made of. Distress tolerance works because the state will end. Acceptance works because you could not hold it anyway.

Duration — *this feeling passes in minutes* — is the smallest observable case, and the doorway. You verify it on one feeling, repeatedly, until you trust it. Then it extends.

## The evidence exists; it is indexed under other words
Earlier note said the evidence was thin. That was a search failure — this literature lives under *nonattachment* and *equanimity*.

- **Sahdra, B. K., Shaver, P. R., & Brown, K. W. (2010). A scale to measure nonattachment: A Buddhist complement to Western research on attachment and adaptive functioning. *Journal of Personality Assessment, 92*(2), 116–127.** The construct is defined as resting on **insight into the constructed and impermanent nature of mental representations** — impermanence is its theoretical foundation. Thirty items drawn from 135 candidates reviewed by nine experts across three major Buddhist traditions. α = .93; test–retest ICC = .869. Meditators score higher. Inversely related to anxious attachment; positively to mindfulness and self-compassion.
- Sahdra's working definition is the one to quote: **a flexible and balanced way of relating to one's experiences without clinging to or suppressing them** — noncontingent wellbeing that does not depend on circumstances, and nonreactivity that supports equanimity under difficulty. That is acceptance and distress tolerance stated as one thing.
- Nonattachment is associated with greater kindness toward self and others, greater compassion, and enhanced wellbeing — evidence against the standard objection that nonattachment means detachment or indifference. **Raise that objection in the essay and answer it with this.**
- **Bhambhani, Y., & Cabral, G. (2016).** Nonattachment and decentering as mediators of the link between mindfulness and psychological distress. Mindfulness and nonattachment were two independent strong predictors of depression, anxiety and stress, **together explaining roughly 25–30% of the variance in each.**
- **Equanimity Scale-16** (*Mindfulness*, 2020). Two factors: **experiential acceptance** and **non-reactivity.** Positively correlated with nonattachment, negatively with distress, and **sensitive to change** — scores rose following a mindfulness-based intervention with concurrent decreases in anxiety, stress and depressive symptoms. The factor structure is the argument: acceptance and distress tolerance falling out of one construct.
- Vipassana, which trains impermanence perception directly by observing sensations arise and pass, has a 2025 PRISMA systematic review covering 2010–2025: promising as an adjunct for stress-related disorders, methodological limitations, larger controlled trials warranted. **Honest limit: outcome evidence for the practice; impermanence-insight itself is not isolated as a measured mediator in what I found.**
