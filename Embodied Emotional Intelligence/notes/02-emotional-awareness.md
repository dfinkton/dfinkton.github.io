## What it's for
Feeling the signal and getting an accurate name on it.

## Practices
Name it, locate it in the body, rate it 1–10. Building granularity — expanding from "bad" to "resentful / excluded / ashamed." Body mapping. Catching the pre-signal.

## Studies
- Lieberman, M. D., et al. (2007). Putting feelings into words. *Psychological Science, 18*(5), 421–428.
- Kashdan, T. B., Barrett, L. F., & McKnight, P. E. (2015). Unpacking emotion differentiation. *Current Directions in Psychological Science, 24*(1), 10–16. — Under intense distress, high-granularity individuals are less likely to resort to binge drinking, aggression, or self-injury; less neural reactivity to rejection; less severe anxiety and depression.
- Pond, R. S., et al. (2012). *Emotion, 12*(2), 326–337. — Daily diary; differentiation moderates aggression in angry people.
- Seah, T. H. S., & Coifman, K. G. (2022). *Emotion, 22*, 1686–1697. — Meta-analysis.
- Granularity increases with intensive repeated labeling — the practice claim.

## Honest notes
Mostly between-subjects correlational. The field is still arguing about incremental validity beyond existing emotion measures.
