## Scope
Attention pointed at your own life. Attention pointed at someone else's good fortune is lesson 30.

## What it's for
A practice everyone recommends, where the evidence says something more specific than the recommendation does.

## Practices
Directing attention toward what is present rather than what is missing. Written and spoken forms. Why frequency may not be the variable.

## Studies
- Cregg, D. R., & Cheavens, J. S. (2021). Gratitude interventions: Effective self-help? *Journal of Happiness Studies, 22*, 413–445. — k = 27, N = 3,675. Small effects on depression and anxiety (g = −0.29 post, −0.23 follow-up). **Medium effect against waitlist, trivial against any active control involving another activity.**
- Davis, D. E., et al. (2016). Thankful for the little things. *Journal of Counseling Psychology, 63*, 20–31. — Concluded that enthusiasm should be tempered until stronger evidence exists.
- Dickens, L. R. (2017). *Basic and Applied Social Psychology, 39*, 193–208.
- Kirca, Malouff & Meynadier (2023). *International Journal of Applied Positive Psychology, 8*, 63–86. — 25 RCTs, 6,745 participants, g = 0.22 vs. neutral comparison.

## The prayer material, which is the stronger evidence for the mechanism
- Poloma, M. M., & Pendleton, B. F. (1989, *Review of Religious Research*; 1991, *Journal of Psychology and Theology, 19*, 71–83). 560 adults, factor analysis, four prayer types. Colloquial (own words, thanking, asking guidance) → greater happiness. Meditative (adoring, reflecting, dwelling in presence) → existential wellbeing. **Petitionary (requesting what is lacking) → no wellbeing association.** Ritual (recitation) → higher negative affect. **Frequency was a weak predictor; type and experience mattered.**
- Maltby et al. (1999) — colloquial and meditative prayer negatively related to anxiety and depression.
- Self-disclosure as mediator (*Journal of Religion and Health*, 2014, N = 296) — mediated colloquial and meditative but not petitionary or ritual. **A competing mechanism with better mediation evidence than gratitude has.**
- Rumination as mediator — petitionary prayer and ritual×stress associated with more rumination and depressive symptoms.

## Negativity bias
- Baumeister, R. F., Bratslavsky, E., Finkenauer, C., & Vohs, K. D. (2001). Bad is stronger than good. *Review of General Psychology, 5*, 323–370.
- Soroka, Fournier & Nir (2019), *PNAS* — stronger physiological arousal to negative news across 17 countries.
- 2024 intergroup contact meta-analysis, 238 samples, N = 152,985 — negative contact effect significantly larger than positive.
- **But** cognitive bias modification meta-analyses show small therapeutic effects with publication bias indications. Direct attention retraining has a weak record.
- Baumeister's own framing: good prevails by force of numbers, not by reweighting the bias.

## Gratitude as a mediator — the finding that changes this lesson
- "An attitude of gratitude: How psychological and social resources mediate the protective effect of religiosity on depressive symptoms." MIDUS 2 (N = 1,052) and MIDUS Refresher (N = 625). **Gratitude and social support fully mediated the relation between religiosity and psychological wellbeing, replicated across both independent national samples.** Cross-sectional — association, not causation.
- *Journal of Happiness Studies* (2021) — gratitude mediated religiosity's relation to life satisfaction and depression across two samples.
- Post-hoc mediation analysis of a six-week gratitude RCT: gratitude at four weeks mediated the intervention's effect on mental wellbeing.
- Pérez, J. E., Rex Smith, A., Norris, R. L., Canenguez, K. M., Tracey, E. F., & DeCristofaro, S. B. (2011). Types of prayer and depressive symptoms among cancer patients: the mediating role of rumination and social support. *Journal of Behavioral Medicine, 34*(6), 519–530. — 179 cancer outpatients. Depressive symptoms correlated negatively with adoration (r = −.15), reception (r = −.17), **thanksgiving (r = −.29)**, and prayer for others (r = −.26). **Rumination fully mediated the link between thanksgiving prayer and depressive symptoms** (β indirect = −.05). The authors' own mechanism: thanksgiving prayer may promote wellbeing "by reducing the level of ruminative self-focused attention, shifting focus away from one's negative affect."
- Black, S. W., Pössel, P., Jeppsen, B. D., Bjerg, A. C., & Wooldridge, D. T. (2014). Disclosure during private prayer as a mediator between prayer type and mental health. *Journal of Religion and Health, 54*(2), 540–553. — N = 296, Preacher & Hayes bootstrapping, Zhao/Lynch/Chen mediation criteria. Self-disclosure fully mediated colloquial and meditative prayer; did not meditate petitionary or ritual. Petitionary showed a negative direct relationship with mental health; ritual a positive one.

## The honest argument, corrected
The *intervention* literature on gratitude is weak — bolt-on exercises barely beat other bolt-on activities. The *mechanism* literature is strong — gratitude mediates religiosity's effect on wellbeing across national samples, and thanksgiving prayer works through reduced ruminative self-focused attention with mediation evidence.

Those are not contradictory, and the difference between them may be the most useful thing in the lesson: **a bolt-on exercise performs differently than an embedded orientation.** That generalizes to everything else in the series.

Two further constraints. Frequency was a weak predictor in Poloma and Pendleton, so "prolonged" is probably the wrong emphasis. And ritual prayer is saturated with thanksgiving content while associating with higher negative affect — so recited gratitude is not sufficient. Self-generated, personally meaningful attention appears to be doing the work.
