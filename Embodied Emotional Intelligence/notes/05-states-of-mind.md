### Conditions
None of this works equally well at all times. Reasoning that is fully available when you are calm is often unavailable when you are not—every adult knows this from the inside, having worked out exactly what they should have said about twenty minutes after the conversation ended.

Certain conditions also weaken the whole system, and most people carry several at once. Sleep loss is the best documented. Alcohol, hunger, long-running stress, and money pressure all measurably reduce the capacity this work runs on. Each gets its own lesson, because each matters more than it looks.

If you have tried to be steadier and failed, this is usually the explanation. Not that you lack the ability—that you were attempting a genuinely difficult skill in conditions that weaken it.
## Panksepp's seven, and state-dependent decisions — relocated 2026-08-13

Belongs in the states lesson rather than the awareness lesson: this is about *which state you are in
changing what you choose*, not about noticing the state.

**Big Five vs the biological systems.** The Big Five (Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion,
Agreeableness, Neuroticism) is **purely descriptive and lexical** — derived by statistically
categorising descriptive words in the dictionary, not by looking at the brain. A 2021 *Scientific
Reports* meta-analysis tested whether Panksepp's biological systems predict it, and found robust,
specific mappings:

| Big Five trait | ANPS primary emotional system |
|---|---|
| Openness to Experience | strongly predicted by high **SEEKING** |
| Extraversion | strongly predicted by high **PLAY** |
| Agreeableness | high **CARE**, low **ANGER** |
| Neuroticism | high **FEAR**, **SADNESS**, **ANGER** |

**Caveat this project has to keep stating:** this is questionnaire-to-questionnaire. It establishes
that two self-report instruments cohere, not that the circuits are doing the work.

**Why the seven are being used anyway** (Darryl's reasoning, recorded because it is a design decision
and not an empirical claim): not because the circuits are biologically settled, but because **the
vocabulary is sufficient to work with**, and can be built on if needed. SEEKING, RAGE, FEAR, LUST,
CARE, PANIC/GRIEF, PLAY. Fewer does not mean fine — it means enough to start.

**The architecture this feeds:** emotion → where it points → how you execute it. Each of the seven
has an action tendency. Anger points toward a boundary, which is why it is not prosocial — it was
never designed to be (see lessons 8 and 11).

**LUST and state-dependent choice — leads, not yet verified.** The claim is that decisions change
with the state you are in, and the literature is large: ovulatory-cycle effects on partner
preference, risk-taking under sexual arousal, tips earned by dancers across the cycle, and
adolescents taking more risk in the presence of other adolescents. **None of these has been read in
primary form for this project. Treat as an unverified research direction, not as citable content.**
The last one (peer presence raising adolescent risk-taking) is the best-established and is Steinberg's
work — reachable via lesson 16.


## Arousal-state identification — Darryl's hypothesis. Belongs here, not in Lesson 2.

**His hypothesis, recorded verbatim:** *"identifying when you are at different arousal states is key."*

**Scope ruling, 13 Aug 2026:** arousal is this lesson's subject. **Lesson 2 is awareness and is
categorical only — the seven names.** The dimensional work (high/low arousal, and what changes about
your decisions at each) is here. I twice tried to fold arousal into Lesson 2 after having already
moved it out; recorded so it doesn't happen a third time.

**What this lesson inherits from the Lesson 2 research pass:**
- The state-dependent decision-making material above (LUST, ovulatory-cycle partner preference, risk
  under arousal, peer presence and adolescent risk-taking). All **unverified leads** — none read in
  primary form.
- One trainability result worth chasing: a **5-day biofeedback trial improved objective heartbeat
  detection.** Objective outcome, not self-report — rare in this literature, and the closest thing to
  evidence that interoceptive precision is trainable at all.
- The warning from [[M1]] applies here too: **objective interoceptive accuracy and self-reported body
  awareness (MAIA) do not converge well.** "Knowing your arousal state" is at least two constructs.
