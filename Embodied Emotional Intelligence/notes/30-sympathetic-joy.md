## Scope
Attention pointed at someone else's good fortune. The inward-facing version is lesson 4.

## Correction
Earlier note said direct evidence was thin. That was wrong. There is a systematic research programme on muditā, published largely by Chinese researchers under the term **appreciative joy** — which is why searching Western affective-science vocabulary returned nothing.

## The measure
Zeng, X., Liao, R., Zhang, R., Oei, T. P., Yao, Z., Leung, F. Y., & Liu, X. (2017). Development of the Appreciative Joy Scale. *Mindfulness, 8*(2), 286–299. **1,622 participants**, EFA and CFA, three factors:
- **Sense of joy** — feeling joy at another's good fortune
- **Positive interpersonal bias** — appreciating and valuing others' virtues
- **Self-transcendence** — feeling appreciative joy *while in one's own inferior situation*; an unenvious attitude

That third factor is the hard case, operationalised: being glad for someone who has what you wanted.

The scale predicted subjective wellbeing and lack of envy after controls, and contributed incrementally to positive emotion, life satisfaction, trait happiness and peace of mind beyond interpersonal relationship quality and beyond the other three immeasurables. Buddhists scored higher than non-religious respondents.

## The intervention
Zeng, X., Wang, R., Oei, T. P., & Leung, F. Y. (2019). Heart of Joy: A randomised controlled trial evaluating the effect of an appreciative joy meditation training on subjective well-being and attitudes. *Mindfulness, 10*(3), 506–515. Training increased appreciative joy and gratitude at one-month follow-up, with **changes in gratitude mediated by appreciative joy.** Subjective wellbeing improved at post-measure, so mediation could not be established for that outcome — state the limit.

## Differentiation, which matters because the specificity claim runs through this series
- Zeng, X., Chan, V. Y., Liu, X., Oei, T. P., & Leung, F. Y. (2017). The four immeasurables meditations: Differential effects of appreciative joy and compassion meditations on emotions. *Mindfulness, 8*(4), 949–959. **Appreciative joy and compassion meditation produce different effects** — the same specificity finding as the ReSource modules, arriving from inside the tradition that separated them first.
- Zeng, X., Chan, V. Y., Oei, T. P., Leung, F. Y., & Liu, X. (2017). Appreciative joy in Buddhism and positive empathy in psychology: How do they differ? *Mindfulness, 8*(5), 1184–1194.
- Appreciative Joy Scale factors predicted acceptance of unfair offers in an Ultimatum Game — a behavioural outcome rather than self-report.
