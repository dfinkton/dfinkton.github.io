## What it's for
The long arc. Everything else in this series operates on minutes and days; this operates on the account you give of your own life, and on where you think you are going.

## Why they are one essay
Purpose is largely narrative-constructed. McAdams and McLean define narrative identity as the internalised, evolving life story that integrates reconstructed past and imagined future to provide **unity, purpose and meaning.** Direction and account are the same operation looking forward and backward. Split them only if the drafting demands it.

## PURPOSE

## The headline evidence
Hill, P. L., & Turiano, N. A. (2014). Purpose in life as a predictor of mortality across adulthood. *Psychological Science, 25*(7), 1482–1486. Using MIDUS, proportional-hazards models found purposeful individuals lived longer over the 14 years after baseline, **controlling for other markers of psychological and affective wellbeing** — and the benefit was not conditional on age, on when during follow-up they died, or on retirement status.

Alongside it: Boyle, P. A., Barnes, L. L., Buchman, A. S., & Bennett, D. A. (2009). Purpose in life is associated with mortality among community-dwelling older persons. *Psychosomatic Medicine, 71*(5), 574–579. · Boyle et al. (2010), *Archives of General Psychiatry, 67*, 304–310 — purpose and reduced risk of incident Alzheimer's disease and mild cognitive impairment.

## The counterweight, and it must be in the essay
Sias and Turtle found the purpose–mortality relation **substantially attenuated or disappeared at longer horizons and when using more comprehensive measures of current health**, concluding that purpose interventions are likely less effective than the existing evidence suggests. And there is strong evidence that **negative health shocks cause a decline in purpose** — a statistically meaningful contemporaneous drop for 12 of 14 health metrics examined. That is reverse causation, and it would produce the observed correlation without purpose protecting anyone.

## A definition worth using
Purpose is narrower than meaning. Meaning is any experience one finds personally significant; purpose is goal-oriented and enduring — a direction, not just a sense of significance.

## In young people
Damon's work on adolescent purpose; Bronk (2011, 2012, 2013); Burrow, A. L., & Hill, P. L. (2011). Purpose as a form of identity capital for positive youth development. *Developmental Psychology, 47*, 1196–1206. Purpose in adolescence is associated with academic efficacy, grit, resilience and internal locus of control, and students with purpose report their schoolwork as more meaningful (Yeager & Bundick, 2009). The Claremont Purpose Scale measures three dimensions and has been validated in adolescents, including in Arabic-language populations. MIDUS data also show **early-life adversity is associated with lower purpose in adulthood**, with age failing to moderate the relationship.

## STORYTELLING

## The finding that makes this essay causal rather than correlational
Adler, J. M. (2012). Living into the story: Agency and coherence in a longitudinal study of narrative identity development and mental health over the course of psychotherapy. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 102*(2), 367–389. Over fifteen weeks of treatment, the theme of **agency** in clients' stories increased — and **those increases temporally preceded the increases in mental health.** The story changed first. Patients whose narratives shifted toward agency, communion and redemption showed better outcomes than patients whose symptoms improved without narrative shift.

That temporal precedence is rare in this field and it is the strongest thing in this entry.

## The two structures to teach
- **Redemption sequence** — a scene that begins badly and is narrated as ending well. Associated with higher wellbeing, generativity and ego development. Highly generative adults disproportionately tell them.
- **Contamination sequence** — something good spoiled by what followed. Associated with depression and lower life satisfaction.

Same events. Different account. Different outcome.

## Also
- McAdams, D. P., & McLean, K. C. (2013). Narrative identity. *Current Directions in Psychological Science, 22*(3), 233–238. Narrators who find redemptive meaning in suffering and whose stories feature personal agency and exploration report higher mental health, wellbeing and maturity.
- Adler, J. M., Lodi-Smith, J., Philippe, F. L., & Houle, I. (2016). The incremental validity of narrative identity in predicting well-being. *Personality and Social Psychology Review, 20*(2), 142–175. Narrative identity predicts wellbeing **beyond** existing measures.
- Bauer & McAdams (2010): communion themes in emerging adults' goal narratives predicted increases in mental health **three years later.**
- **Directly on this series' subject:** meaning-making during high- and low-point life story episodes has been found to predict **emotion regulation two years later.**
- Narrative therapy (White & Epston, 1990) — externalising the problem, locating unique outcomes where the problem did not dominate, re-authoring. Evidence base is **growing but smaller than CBT**; RCTs show benefits for depression, PTSD and family conflict, and it is particularly used in cross-cultural and community settings.

## The connection to essay 25
This is the same mechanism as *Talk About It*, at a longer timescale. Disclosure organises an episode; narrative identity organises a life. Both work through articulation and coherence rather than through discharge. Cross-reference them.

## Honest limits
- McAdams and McLean state the field's own gaps: causal relations between narrative features and adaptation are not disentangled, and cultural context is under-explored. Adler's temporal-precedence finding is the exception, not the norm.
- **Cultural master narratives constrain which stories are available.** The redemption sequence is a notably American form. A reader whose circumstances do not resolve should not be told their story is the wrong shape. Write this carefully — the risk here is prescribing a redemptive frame to someone in an unredeemed situation, which is a way of telling them their suffering is a failure of narration.

## Practices
Writing the account rather than replaying it. Looking for agency in episodes where you felt you had none. Noticing whether you habitually tell contamination sequences. Naming the direction you are actually moving in, distinct from the one you would claim.
