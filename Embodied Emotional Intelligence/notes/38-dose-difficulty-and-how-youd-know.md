## Curriculum-design findings that apply to every lesson — relocated 2026-08-13

These were sitting in the Lesson 2 register but they are not about emotional awareness. They govern
how any of the 42 lessons should be built.

- **Dose does not work the way anyone assumes. Eight within-study nulls or reversals** across
  independent literatures, including **two pre-registered hypotheses that failed in the opposite
  direction**: Cipriano found programs longer than 16 weeks did **worse**; Ball found nothing across
  1–45 minutes. Sprung's emotion-understanding training: sessions and training period were **null
  moderators at all three outcome levels**. What did matter instead: **spacing, session length, and
  sequence.**
- **Sequence is the one thing that landed. Cipriano et al., 424 studies, N=575,361: emotion skills
  before social skills** (school climate g=0.814).
- **Short, stripped-down trainings are not weaker than long omnibus programs** on tested emotion
  outcomes.
- **Targeted roughly doubles universal** (Payton: conduct 0.47, distress 0.50 vs 0.23) — but it is
  K-8, not peer-reviewed, and **unmonitored studies reported the largest effects (0.71)**.
- **What a program contains depends on the codebook, not the field.** PracticeWise says emotional
  awareness is absent; EASEL ranks it 3rd of 116. Boustani's element frequencies: problem solving
  **76%**, anger management **12%**, self-monitoring **9%**.
- **Hold that against Compas:** problem solving is **null on 45 studies and 27,447 youth** — while
  taking 30% of prevention-program sessions.
