## The Evidence

**People who can tell their unpleasant feelings apart are less aggressive on the days they are angriest.** Three separate studies followed college students through several weeks of daily diaries, each day recording both their emotions and their behavior. Researchers scored each person on how finely they told their negative emotions apart — whether their answers actually moved with what was happening to them that day, rather than describing every bad day the same way — then compared people who scored high on that measure to people who scored low, on the specific days each person was angriest. On those days, the people who told their feelings apart most precisely acted less aggressively than the people who didn't: 17% less in the first study, 43% less in the second, and 16% less in the third ([Pond and colleagues, 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22023359/)). Nobody was taught anything here — this compares people who already differ, not the effect of a practice.
